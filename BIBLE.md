# SKY GARDEN: Design Bible v0.1

*A cozy life sim on floating islands. Farm the clouds, befriend the neighbors, and stitch a broken sky village back together, one bridge at a time.*

---

## 1. The Pitch

Long ago the Sky Village kept the **Sky Library**: every discovery people ever made, stored as memories in the islands themselves. The Great Gust broke the village apart and scattered the memories into the dirt, the stars, and the wind.

Your grandmother was the last **Keeper of Memory**. She left you her hut, a hoe, and a letter: *"The sky remembers what it used to be. Help it."*

You farm, dig, and help your neighbors. Along the way you keep doing strange little tasks: watching a shadow, tuning chimes, counting notches. Each one turns out to be a real discovery from human history. When you finish, a memory returns, a neighbor tells you the true story, and the islands drift a little closer.

**The hook in one line:** *Every bridge you build brings a piece of home back, and every memory you find is a real piece of ours.*

## 1b. The Miyagi Rule (how learning works)

Wax on, wax off. **Do it first, understand it later.**

1. **Do:** a small, playable task that feels like a chore or a puzzle (find noon with a shadow, pick chimes that sound nice).
2. **Aha:** after you succeed, a card reveals what you actually did: "That is how Eratosthenes measured the whole Earth."
3. **Today:** one line on where you meet this in real life.
4. **Journal:** every Aha card goes in your Memory Journal to reread.

Never a quiz. Never a wall of text before play. No wrong answers on philosophy questions. Facts stay honest: when scholars disagree or a story is a legend, we say so.

**Topic threads** (each island leans on one, all mix freely):
- **Ancient and archaeology:** dig spots with real layers (deeper is older). Ishango bone, Gobekli Tepe, Babylonian tablets, the Antikythera mechanism.
- **Math:** base 60, ratios, the golden spiral in sunflowers (crop layouts), Zeno's halves, prime numbers in cicada cycles.
- **Science:** Eratosthenes' shadow, star navigation (Polynesian wayfinding on Night Isle), water cycle (rain), seeds and pollinators.
- **Philosophy:** Pip's Big Question each day: Ship of Theseus, Zeno, Plato's Cave, Heraclitus. Later: Confucius, Stoics, Ibn Sina's Floating Man (literally floating, perfect for the sky).
- **Religion and belief:** respectful and curious, never preachy. Ancient temples, sky and world-tree myths from many cultures (Yggdrasil, Mount Meru, Polynesian sky stories), and harvest festivals from many traditions (Sukkot, Pongal, Chuseok, Thanksgiving, Mid-Autumn) as seasonal island festivals.

## 1c. Chapter 1: The Wind Bell (built in v0.2)

1. Read Grandma's letter. Talk to Nana Gale.
2. Dig up 3 sparkly spots: the Counting Bone, the Oldest Temple carving, a Babylonian tablet.
3. Tap the sundial when its shadow is shortest (noon). Aha: Eratosthenes.
4. Tune the Wind Bell: pick chimes whose lengths sound sweet. Aha: Pythagoras and ratios.
5. The bell rings. Far away, another bell answers. Fix the bridge to find out who.

## 2. Pillars (every feature must serve at least one)

1. **Gentle progress you can see.** The map literally grows. Bridges are the big visible goal.
2. **Neighbors who remember you.** Friendship changes what people say and what they give you.
3. **The sky is alive.** Weather, wind, and time of day change what you can do, not just how it looks.
4. **Short sessions feel whole.** One in-game day is about 4 minutes. A phone session of 10 minutes finishes something.
5. **Toy-box charm.** Everything looks like a chunky, rounded toy you want to pick up.

## 3. Core Loop

**Day:** wake up, water, harvest, sell, talk, spend.
**Week:** save up for seeds, gifts, and a bridge.
**Season:** a new island, new crops, a festival.

## 5. Islands (the map is the progression)

1. **Home Isle** (start): your hut, a 3x3 plot, Pip, Nana Gale.
2. **Orchard Isle** (bridge: 150 coins): fruit trees, Captain Drizzle, the twins, fishing.
3. **Windmill Isle**: turns wind into power, unlocks sprinklers.
4. **Night Isle**: only reachable after dark, glowing crops, Lumen.
5. **Market Isle**: Pip's real shop, a museum, festivals.
6. **The Old Heart** (endgame): the center of the old village. Ring the Wind Bell.

## 8. Art Direction: Chunky 3D Toy

- Everything built from soft rounded shapes. No sharp edges on anything friendly.
- Limited palette per island. Home Isle: grass mint, dirt cocoa, sky peach to periwinkle.
- Characters are two spheres plus ears. Big eyes, pink cheeks. Readable at phone size.
- Shadows soft. Night is deep blue, never black.
- Upgrade path: prototype shapes now, swap to modeled .glb toys (Blender or AI 3D) once the feel is locked.

## 9. Music and Sound

- **Rule:** the sky is ambient weather, never harsh hits. Soft pads, wind chimes, rain hush.
- Morning: bright major pad. Afternoon: warm. Evening: gentle minor 7th. Night: slow, sparse chime.
- Each island gets its own instrument layer: Orchard = kalimba, Windmill = accordion, Night = glass harmonica.
- Actions make tiny soft sounds (till = soft thump, harvest = little bloom).

## 10. Writing Voice

- Instructions: plain and short. "Tap dirt to plant."
- Neighbor lines: warm, specific, a little funny. Nobody is mean. Everybody is a little lonely.

## 11. Rollout Plan

## 12. Long-Term Vision: A Guided Adventure Life Sim

**One line:** your own story, a shared sky, and eventually a place to build and sell.

**Layer 1: Your own journey.** Your island, garden, hut, collections, and friendships, guided by chapters so it never feels aimless. Stays single-player at heart.

**Layer 2: Shared experiences.** Optional, cozy, never competitive:
- Visit friends' islands (walk around, leave a gift, water their crops).
- Community goals with a shared progress bar and rewards for everyone.
- Shared real-date festivals (everyone's lanterns in one sky).
- A village board for requests and trades.
- Seasonal community mysteries: clues unlock as the whole community solves them.

**Layer 3: Creators and businesses.** Player-made items (furniture, clothes, decor) reviewed and sold for coins or real money; a Market Isle with stalls for small businesses and artists; musicians and artists featured in festivals, the Library, and the Temple Garden.

**Build order:** journey, then shared world, then creators.

**Plan for now, build later:** real accounts, safety and moderation (reporting, review, filters; COPPA if kids play), payments (Stripe or similar, payouts, refunds, fraud; legal review first), and a quality review queue so creations fit the cozy style.

**Safety principle:** shared features start with friendly actions only (gifts, watering, waves), no open chat.

## 13. Real Life First

Everything you grow, pick, catch, cook, and collect is a real thing with its real name (Wheat, Pumpkin, Kale, Rainbow Trout, Manta Ray), with sizes and seasons as close to real life as the game allows. Every item card has two parts: **In real life** (a checked, true fact) and **In Sky Garden** (the game's rules, like growing in 2 days or only biting in the rain). Magic stays obviously magic: floating islands, a bridge of light, a firefly painter.

## 14. The Blend: Minecraft, Lego, Stardew, Animal Crossing, Zelda, Hello Kitty, South Park

- **Minecraft:** start with almost nothing; gather, craft tools, build anything.
- **Lego games:** snap-together building pieces and playful physical humor.
- **Stardew Valley:** farming, seasons, a purpose every day.
- **Animal Crossing:** neighbors with personality, decorating, real-time days and festivals.
- **Zelda:** exploration; new tools open new places; puzzles, secrets, caves.
- **Hello Kitty:** cuteness, friendship, gifts, outfits, collectibles.
- **South Park:** funny, sharp writing adults laugh at too. Never crude or mean; always kid-safe.

**Crafting follows real human history:** stone tools and fire, pottery, bronze (copper plus tin), glass, iron, windmills. Each new material is a real "aha."

**Rollout:** (1) start from nothing and build your home [built], (2) free building anywhere on a snap grid, (3) the history tech tree, (4) exploration: wild islands and caves opened by tools.
